# Angioplasty and stents.
A narrowed heart artery is opened from the inside through a thin tube, often with a small mesh called a stent. Here is how it works, and when bypass may suit you better.

## Before, during and after.

### Getting ready
- Tests: ECG, echo, blood tests and, if not done, an angiography.
- Medicines: blood thinners are often started or adjusted before.
- The plan: why a stent, why not bypass, and what the risks are, explained first.

### The procedure
- You stay awake, with a local anaesthetic at the wrist or groin.
- A thin tube is guided to the heart artery under X-ray.
- A small balloon opens the narrowing, and a stent usually holds it open.
- You may feel pressure, but it is usually not painful.

### Recovery
- A short stay for observation, with the puncture site checked.
- Blood thinners every day, exactly as advised, to protect the stent.
- Most daily activities return gradually, guided at follow-up.
- Rehab and lifestyle changes help protect your arteries.

## FAQs

**Stent or bypass, which is right for me?**
It depends on how many arteries are narrowed, where, and your other health conditions. Dr. Yadav is a heart surgeon who explains both options plainly, so you can decide with the full picture.

**Will I be awake?**
Yes, usually. A local anaesthetic numbs the wrist or groin and you may be given something to relax.

**Can the artery narrow again?**
It can happen in some people. Taking your medicines and controlling risk factors lowers the chance, and follow-up visits help catch it early.

**How soon can I go home?**
Often within a short stay, depending on how the procedure went and your overall health. You will get clear instructions before discharge.
