# Vascular surgery.
Blocked arteries in the legs, swollen veins, weak bulging arteries called aneurysms and other vessel problems. Here is how they are treated, from medicines to surgery.

## Tick what sounds like you.

## Before, during and after.

### Finding the problem
- Tests: Doppler scan, CT angiography and blood tests as needed.
- Options: medicines, walking programmes, a procedure or surgery, explained side by side.
- Your decision: taken with your family once you understand the plan.

### The treatment
- Depending on the problem, this may be a day procedure or an operation.
- A blocked artery may be bypassed with a graft or opened from inside.
- Veins may be treated to stop pain and swelling.
- You will know beforehand which anaesthesia is used.

### Recovery
- Walking is usually encouraged early.
- Wound and foot care are taught before you go home.
- Medicines to protect the vessels, taken every day.
- Regular follow-up scans where needed.

## FAQs

**Does leg pain on walking always need surgery?**
No. Many people improve with medicines, stopping smoking and a walking plan. Surgery or a procedure is advised when these are not enough.

**Are varicose veins dangerous?**
Often they cause aching and swelling. Sometimes they lead to skin changes or ulcers, which need treatment.

**What is an aneurysm?**
It is a weak, bulging part of an artery. Small ones are watched with scans, larger ones may need repair.

**Why does diabetes matter for my feet?**
Diabetes can affect both the blood supply and feeling in the feet, so small wounds need early attention.
