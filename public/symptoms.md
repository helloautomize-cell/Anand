# What is your body telling you?
Chest pain, breathlessness, a racing heart or leg pain on walking can all start in the heart or blood vessels. Find what you feel, learn what it may mean, and know when to act fast.

## Your body often speaks first.
Choose what you feel to see where it starts in the body and what it may mean. General information, not a diagnosis.

## Call 108 or 112 if you notice this.
These signs need emergency care, not a clinic appointment. Tap a card to see what to do.

### Chest pain lasting more than a few minutes
Call 108 or 112. Sit down and rest. Do not drive yourself to hospital.

### Sudden, severe breathlessness
Call 108 or 112. Sit upright and loosen tight clothing while you wait.

### Fainting or collapse
Call 108 or 112. Lay the person flat. If they are not breathing, start chest compressions.

### Face drooping, weak arm, slurred speech
These can be signs of a stroke. Call 108 or 112 and note the time the signs began.

### A cold, pale, painful leg
A sudden blocked leg artery needs urgent care. Call 108 or 112 or go to the nearest emergency department.
In any emergency, go to the nearest hospital emergency department. The clinic line is for appointments and is not an emergency service.

## Tests that tell the real story.
Only the tests you need are advised. Hover or tap a card to see what to expect.

### The electrical picture of your heart
Small stickers on the chest record each heartbeat in a few minutes. It can show rhythm problems and signs of strain or an old heart attack.
Painless and quick. No preparation needed.

### Ultrasound of the heart
Shows the pump, the valves and the walls moving.
A gel and a probe on the chest. Takes about as long as a short scan.

### Treadmill test
Checks how the heart copes with effort.
You walk on a treadmill while the ECG is recorded. Wear comfortable shoes.

### A map of the heart arteries
Dye and X-ray show exactly where an artery is narrowed.
Done in a cath lab at the hospital through the wrist or groin, usually while you are awake.

### Blood flow in the legs and neck
An ultrasound that shows narrowed arteries or leaking veins.
Painless. Wear loose clothes so the legs are easy to reach.

### Detailed pictures of the chest and vessels
Used for the aorta, the lungs and sometimes the heart arteries.
Some scans use a dye. Tell the team about kidney problems or allergies.

## What people often believe.

### Heart attack pain is always severe and sudden.
It can feel like mild pressure, gas, or pain in the jaw, arm or back. Women, older people and people with diabetes often have milder signs.

### Young people do not get heart disease.
Heart disease is appearing at younger ages in India. Smoking, diabetes, high BP and family history raise the risk at any age.

### If my ECG is normal, my heart is fine.
A resting ECG can be normal between episodes. Sometimes an echo, a TMT or other tests are needed to find the cause.

### Leg pain on walking is just age.
Calf pain that comes with walking and eases with rest can mean narrowed leg arteries. It is worth checking.

### Varicose veins are only a cosmetic problem.
They can cause aching, swelling, skin changes and ulcers, and they can be treated.

### After a stent or bypass, I can stop my medicines.
Medicines protect the stent or grafts. Never stop them without asking your doctor.

## FAQs

**Is every chest pain from the heart?**
No. Chest pain can come from muscles, the stomach, the lungs or anxiety. Because it can be the heart, new or unexplained chest pain should always be checked.

**When should I see a heart surgeon rather than a physician?**
Many people start with a physician or cardiologist. You can see Dr. Yadav directly if you have been told you may need surgery, want a second opinion, or have a vessel or chest problem.

**Can anxiety cause palpitations?**
Yes, but palpitations can also come from rhythm problems. If they are frequent or come with dizziness or fainting, get them checked.

**I have diabetes. Should I be more careful?**
Yes. Diabetes raises the risk of heart and vessel disease, and symptoms can be milder. Regular check-ups are a good idea.

**What should I bring to my appointment?**
All earlier reports, your medicines, and a short note of your symptoms: when they started, how long they last, and what brings them on.
